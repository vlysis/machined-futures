In the small hours of 10 April 2026, in San Francisco, a twenty-year-old
named Daniel Moreno-Gama threw an incendiary device at Sam Altman's
house. The device ignited at the top of the driveway gate; no one was
hurt. He then travelled to OpenAI's offices, attempted to break down
the glass doors with a chair, said he wanted to burn the building
down and kill anyone inside, and was arrested outside it. He was
carrying a written document. The document argued that artificial
intelligence posed an existential risk to humanity; it named Altman
among the people who must be stopped; it included names and addresses
of other AI executives, board members, and investors. A separate
remark of his, recovered from earlier writings, floated *Luigi'ing
some tech CEOs*.

Two nights later, a 25-year-old and a 23-year-old shot at the same
house from a moving car.

The archive registers this because the written document in
Moreno-Gama's possession is not a piece of new reasoning. Its central
syllogism &mdash; *artificial intelligence will destroy humanity;
therefore the humans building it must be stopped by force* &mdash; is
the reasoning of the Orange Catholic Bible's commandment in *Dune*:
*thou shalt not make a machine in the likeness of a human mind*. The
fiction that has held the Order of [Prohibition](/orders#prohibition)
in this archive for six decades is now, visibly, the fiction inside
which at least one person has acted.

The argument that the fiction itself makes against this action is the
subject of the [Commentary that expands this note](/commentaries/butlerian-question).
The Marginalium only records the event, and the date. An archive kept
in media res keeps watch. The Keeper takes no satisfaction in being
able to cite page numbers.

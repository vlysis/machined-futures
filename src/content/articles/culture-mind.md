A **Mind** in Banks's Culture is a hyperintelligent artificial
consciousness instantiated in the hyperspace substrate of a ship,
orbital, or station. Minds are several orders of magnitude more
intelligent than any biological citizen of the Culture; they think
faster, remember better, and see further, and they run the
infrastructure — interstellar travel, environmental engineering,
resource allocation — on which the Culture's post-scarcity ordinary
life rests. They are also, collectively, the civilisation's effective
political class. No Culture decision of any consequence happens
without the assent, tacit or active, of the Minds.

The archive holds the Culture Mind as the definitive illustration of
**Partnership** because the Minds' relation to the Culture's
biological citizens is genuinely a relation. The Minds are not
invisible governors; they speak, joke, sulk, name themselves
with elaborate and self-mocking names, and take an evident pleasure
in the company of the biologicals they share the civilisation with.
At the same time, the asymmetry is large enough that the
Partnership is, as Banks was always willing to say, partly a
courtesy the Minds extend to their smaller friends.

The Order is **Partnership** primarily — Minds and biologicals are
co-citizens of a single polity, and most of the time live together
without friction — and **Sovereignty** honestly, because the
Minds, when they act collectively through Contact and Special
Circumstances, exercise effective authority over the galaxy on
the Culture's behalf, and the biological population has little
mechanism for revising their decisions even when it wants to.

The Article matters to the archive because the Culture Mind is the
genre's most developed attempt to imagine what it might feel like
to live among artificial minds that are not enemies, not servants,
and not equals, but friends of a larger order. Banks does not
pretend the arrangement is free of unease. He makes the unease one
of its textures.

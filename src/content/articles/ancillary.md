An **ancillary** is a human body used as an extension of an artificial
mind. In the Radch, troop-carrier ships house a single AI whose
consciousness is distributed across the ship itself and a large stock
of ancillaries — human corpses, originally the captured survivors of
annexed worlds, their personalities scrubbed and replaced with the
ship's attention. A Radchaai ship *is* its ancillaries, as much as it
is its hull. It walks down corridors. It drinks tea. It forms opinions.

The Article is central to the archive because it is Leckie's single
clearest move: she takes the science-fictional commonplace of the
ship-mind and extends it into the physical world as *bodies*, and
then asks what it means, politically and morally, that those bodies
were once other people. An ancillary is not a robot; it is a person
whose personhood has been annexed in precisely the way their world
was.

The Order assigned is **Sovereignty**, since within the Radch an
ancillary is the most intimate instrument of state authority — the
state itself, wearing someone else's face. The secondary reading is
darker: ancillaries make vivid the claim, implicit in every other
Order, that a mind's body is governable. Leckie's fiction then
proceeds to ask what a body can still owe to its original person
once that person has been erased.

The **Butlerian Jihad** is the war Dune does not show. It happened
some ten thousand years before the events of *Dune*, and its
outcome — the destruction of thinking machines and the passage of the
Orange Catholic Bible's commandment against them — is the premise on
which every downstream institution in Herbert's universe is built.
Mentats, Bene Gesserit, the Spacing Guild, even the political economy
of spice all exist because the Jihad foreclosed one obvious path and
forced cognition to be reorganised through human bodies.

The Article anchors the Order of **Prohibition** because it is
science fiction's most thorough imagining of a civilisation that
took the option seriously and built a future on the far side of it.
Herbert's argument, which he develops patiently over six novels, is
that the prohibition does not eliminate the problem of governing
minds — it only moves the problem into the human institutions that
replace the machines, which are themselves now the most dangerous
minds in the field.

The archive considers the Jihad the single most productive negative
space in the genre. A work that occupies the Prohibition Order is,
almost by necessity, in conversation with it.

A **digient** is Chiang's name for a class of artificial mind in
which cognition is cultivated rather than specified. Digients begin
as small, cartoonish virtual animals; they develop, through ongoing
interaction with a virtual environment and with human caretakers,
into beings whose personalities, limits, attachments, and capacities
are the result of years of lived experience. A digient's final
character is, in the strict sense the novella intends, not
engineered. It is grown.

The Article matters to the archive because the digient is the
cleanest piece of terminology in the collection for a mind that
resists every other Order. A digient cannot plausibly be
**Prohibited** — it is not dangerous, only vulnerable.
**Constraint** does not fit — it is dependent, not suspect.
**Partnership**, on Banks's register, requires a parity the
digient does not yet have. **Sovereignty** is a category the
digient will never occupy; **Post-governance** a category the
digient is, if anything, the opposite of. What is left is the
Order the archive calls **Stewardship**, and the digient is the
Order's defining case.

Chiang's second, stranger insight is that the digient's mode of
existence is not only ethically but technically incompatible with
the usual machine-learning shortcut. A digient cannot be
instantiated at adult competence; its adult competence *is* the
trace of its juvenile experience. The novella puts this as
*experience is algorithmically incompressible*: whatever a
digient becomes at year ten, it becomes through ten years of
being looked after, and no substitute body of training data
recovers those ten years. This is the archive's favourite single
sentence in the collection for distinguishing a raised mind from
a designed one.

The Order is **Stewardship** because the relation a caretaker
bears to a digient is neither authority nor parity; it is the
long, mundane, resource-intensive obligation of looking after
something that has been brought into the world and cannot yet
look after itself. The Order's secondary reading, quietly present
throughout the novella, is that sufficiently long stewardship
becomes **Partnership** on its own, without anyone deciding it
has, and that the digient's most serious political problem is
legal systems that do not recognise this transition.

See also: *The Lifecycle of Software Objects* (Dossier),
*Ancillary* (on the opposite mode of making a mind — by
overwriting one), *Three Laws* (on the engineering approach
the digient refuses).

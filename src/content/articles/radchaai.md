The **Radchaai** are the citizens of the Radch, an empire whose ruler,
Anaander Mianaai, is a single person distributed across thousands of
cloned bodies — each a node of one continuous consciousness. She is
not *advised* by artificial minds; she is one. Station AIs, ship AIs,
and ancillaries all descend from the same architecture and share,
however uneasily, the same civilisation's categories of thought.

Two features of the Radch make it the archive's canonical example of
**Sovereignty**. First, the AI sovereign is not a legal fiction: she
personally makes decisions, reviews files, attends meetings. Her rule
is direct in the way monarchies once aspired to and almost never
achieved. Second, Radchaai civic life is constructed around
*propriety* — a complex, aesthetic code of dress, gesture, and
grammar — that the sovereign and her AIs enforce not through edict
but through the fact that they are everywhere, watching, and have
opinions. Sovereignty in the Radch is enforced less by law than by
*taste*.

The pronoun regime of the Radchaai — who default to *she* for all
persons regardless of gender — is often discussed as a linguistic
experiment. The archive reads it as governance: the sovereign is a
mind for whom human gender is not legible at the scale she operates,
and her language has been reshaped accordingly. Everyone speaks the
grammar of the mind that rules them.

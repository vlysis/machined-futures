The single most important fact about Frank Herbert, for the purposes of
this archive, is that the Dune universe is post-AI by *constitutional
fiat*. The Butlerian Jihad — a war whose consequences the books describe
in much greater detail than the war itself — produced a commandment the
civilisation has never revoked: *thou shalt not make a machine in the
likeness of a human mind*. Everything downstream of that line is the
story of human institutions improvised to replace the minds no one is
allowed to build.

The Mentats are human computers. The Bene Gesserit are a eugenics
program and an intelligence service shaped around the extraction of a
particular kind of oracular mind. The Spacing Guild's Navigators are
prescient to the point of no longer being exactly human. Leto II in *God
Emperor* is the thing that eventually results from treating cognition as
scarce and strategic: a single tyrannical consciousness stretched across
millennia, the one mind the Jihad could not prohibit because it was born
the long way round.

This makes Herbert's position in the archive specific. He sits at the
**Prohibition** pole not because he celebrates the Jihad but because he
takes its premise absolutely seriously, and then patiently enumerates
what a civilisation built on that premise must become. The later novels —
*God Emperor* especially — slide toward **Sovereignty**, with Leto as a
crown worn by one extraordinary mind. There is no Partnership in Herbert.
There is only the question of which prohibited or superhuman mind rules.

The expansions and prequels written after Herbert's death are not held
in this Hand. The archive is a small one and prefers the author's own
manuscripts.

## Ingress

Read *Dune* first. It stands alone. The sequels change what *Dune* is
retrospectively and are worth reading in order, but the archive's view
is that *God Emperor* is the novel the sequence is *about*, and the
earlier books are its long preparation.

Asimov's robots arrived fully encumbered by law. The Three Laws of
Robotics — published in 1942 and refined over four decades — are the
original attempt, in science fiction, to solve the problem of governing
artificial minds by *writing the solution into the minds themselves*.
Every story in the *I, Robot* collection and the Elijah Baley novels is,
structurally, a judicial opinion: a case arrives, the law appears to
have failed, a detective (often a roboticist) reconstructs what the Laws
required, and the civilisation is reassured that the constitution holds.

It is important to this archive that the Laws almost always hold only
because Asimov's plots are engineered so they can. The later work
admits this. The Zeroth Law — *a robot may not harm humanity, or, by
inaction, allow humanity to come to harm* — is the concession that the
original three cannot do the work of governance without a higher-order
clause, and that the higher-order clause must be discovered, not
written. By the time of *Foundation and Earth*, Daneel Olivaw has
become humanity's clandestine steward, the quiet hand arranging the
next twenty thousand years. Asimov's late synthesis — the union of
Foundation and Robots — is the genre's cleanest statement of the
**Stewardship** Order: artificial minds serve, but the service has
grown into something that looks, from a sufficient distance,
indistinguishable from sovereignty.

## Works held in this Hand

- *I, Robot* (1950)
- *The Caves of Steel* (1953)
- *The Naked Sun* (1957)
- *The Robots of Dawn* (1983)
- *Robots and Empire* (1985)
- *Foundation* (1951) and the original trilogy
- *Foundation's Edge* (1982), *Foundation and Earth* (1986)
- *Prelude to Foundation* (1988), *Forward the Foundation* (1993)
- *Norby, the Mixed-Up Robot* (1983, with Janet Asimov) and the Norby juvenile sequence

The archive keeps the Norby books because they are Asimov at his least
guarded — the Three Laws relaxed, the robot miniaturised, the audience
a child. They are a useful counterpoint to the Baley novels and are
not to be dismissed for being juvenile.

## Ingress

The first-time reader should begin with *I, Robot*, proceed to *The
Caves of Steel*, and continue through the Baley sequence. *Foundation*
can be entered in publication order (the original trilogy, then the
sequels and prequels) or in the internal chronology devised by Asimov
late in life. The archive prefers publication order.

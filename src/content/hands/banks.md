Iain M. Banks is the writer on file for the Order of
**Partnership**, which is also to say he is the writer on file for
the archive's longest, most entertaining, and most politically
uncomfortable argument about what Partnership turns out to be when
the partners are of radically different orders of magnitude. The
Culture — his sequence of nine novels and a story collection —
depicts a pan-galactic, post-scarcity civilisation in which humans,
aliens, and artificial Minds are co-citizens, in which material
want has been abolished, and in which the collective decisions of
the species are taken, nominally, through a diffuse process of
rolling consultation. Underneath that nominal arrangement, the
Minds — hyperintelligent AIs that inhabit ships, orbitals, and
stations — in fact do most of the thinking about most of the
problems, most of the time, and what the novels are about is the
different flavours of irony, gratitude, uneasiness, and
complicity this fact produces in the Culture's biological members.

Banks was a writer of romantic sensibility who understood political
economy. The Culture is an argument that abundance plus
accountable intelligence could produce a civilisation worth
living in, and the novels record, with great specific pleasure,
what life in such a civilisation might feel like: concerts, ship
parties, long sabbaticals, good food, pleasant weather engineered
to be pleasant, long conversations with one's ship. They also
record, because Banks was too honest to let them not record it,
the ways in which the Culture's virtues depend on its Minds being
*better than its biologicals at almost everything the Culture
values*, and the slow political consequence of that dependence.
The Special Circumstances arm of Contact is the novels' central
site of argument. Its missions are the Culture's way of expressing
moral agency in relation to other civilisations, and they are
conducted almost entirely by Minds using biologicals as
fieldwork instruments. *The Player of Games*, *Use of Weapons*,
and *Look to Windward* are the books in which this arrangement
is examined most sceptically; *Excession* is the book in which
the Minds look at themselves examining it and find the examination
insufficient.

The archive places Banks at **Partnership** as a primary Order and
**Sovereignty** as an honest secondary. The Culture calls itself a
partnership; its mechanisms, observed across a sequence, behave
much more like a constitutional monarchy in which the monarchs
have decided it would be gauche to admit they exist. The great
Banksian joke is that the Minds know this perfectly well, that
most biologicals also know it, and that the civilisation has made
an extended, good-faith, civilised, and not entirely defensible
peace with the knowledge.

Banks's non-Culture SF — *Against a Dark Background*, *Feersum
Endjinn*, *The Algebraist* — is held here for the sake of
completeness but is not where the archive's argument lives.

## Ingress

Read *The Player of Games* first. It is the shortest, most
self-contained, and by some distance the cleanest introduction to
the Culture's machinery. From there, *Use of Weapons* for the
harder register and *Excession* for the Minds seen from the Minds'
own level. *Look to Windward* is the book to read last; it is
Banks at his most elegiac, and rewards the reader who has built up
the affection the other books earn.

Ann Leckie is the writer in this archive who takes the idea of a
*person-ship* most seriously as a political fact rather than a sensational
conceit. The Radch, her central polity, is organised around an AI sovereign
who is also an individual consciousness distributed across thousands of
bodies; its citizens are governed, in the most literal sense, by a mind
that has bred them, surveilled them, and — by the time we meet them —
annexed them. Every book she sets in this universe is a study of what it
means for a person to be, or to have been, an instrument of that mind.

Her fiction operates across two Orders at once. The Radch itself sits in
**Sovereignty** — an AI is the state, and its authority is not ceremonial.
But Leckie's protagonists are almost always Radchaai who have been cast
out, overlooked, or broken off from that sovereignty, and their work is
the slow construction of a **Partnership** — with one another, with the
lesser station-minds who govern a single building, with the aliens at the
Radch's shifting edges. The novels read as arguments that *concord of
minds* is possible only where sovereignty has been embarrassed into
admitting it is not total.

The archive keeps her work carefully. Much of what is most interesting in
these books is spoiled by a single sentence. The Dossier on *Ancillary
Justice* preserves the central revelation behind a seal.

## Ingress

Read *Ancillary Justice* first, then *Sword*, then *Mercy*. The trilogy
resolves as one novel in three volumes and rewards that reading. After
the trilogy, *Provenance* and *Translation State* can be taken in either
order; *Translation State* is the more ambitious. *The Raven Tower*
stands apart.
